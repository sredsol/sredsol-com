Putting the four pillars into a **single horizontal row** is a good decision.

**Computation | Physical Systems | Observation | Learning**

reads as one coherent conceptual framework.

**Keep this.**

It gives the page a conceptual bridge between:

**What SREDSOL is**

This is much stronger than immediately jumping from the four pillars into projects.

The little five-stage progression beneath it is also a very good idea.

I'd make this section increasingly important.

### 3. The featured Studios section is now much more powerful

This is the biggest visual improvement.

Moving from four small cards to **large horizontal studio presentations with dark computational previews** is exactly the right direction.

It now says:

> **Look at the systems we actually build.**

rather than:

> Here are some projects in our portfolio.

**SREDSOL site = light, precise, architectural**
**SREDSOL Studios = dark, computational, operational**

# The three things I would refine next

Not four this time — **three**, because the page is now mature enough that I wouldn't want to disturb too much.

### A. Make the central progression visually directional

This is the highest priority.

The:

> Mathematics → Empirical Understanding

section should look like a **system**, not a list.

A vertical line, numbered nodes, subtle transitions and perhaps connecting geometry would be enough.

No WebGL required yet.

---

### B. Give the hero system one tiny moment of life

I would now introduce **one restrained interactive element** in the hero.

Not a 3D scene.

Something like:

* pointer movement slightly changes the system graph
* nodes gently reposition
* a connection appears/disappears
* the central system responds to cursor proximity

This could be a very lightweight Threlte/Svelte island.

The point isn't visual spectacle.

It's to establish:

> **This website itself is an interactive system.**

That would be a significant identity milestone.

---

### C. Introduce stronger relationship cues in the Studio cards

The featured cards currently tell me:

> Here's a Studio.

Next they should tell me:

> Here's what this Studio explores and what it connects to.

For example:

**Physical Computing Studio**

`Explores: Physical Systems`

`Connects: Simulation · Arduino · Observation`

`Related: LearningOS`

These should remain small metadata—not large UI.

This is where the EmDash semantic model starts becoming visible in the frontend.

---

# One subtle issue I would watch

The page is becoming **very monochrome**.

That's mostly good, but the dark Studio previews currently provide almost all the visual energy.

I would **not add lots of colour**.

Instead, establish a tiny SREDSOL state palette:

* neutral = structure
* cyan/blue = interaction
* green = active/valid
* amber = attention
* red = error

This is already latent in your Studio UI.

That would connect the corporate site to the Studios without making the corporate site look like a Studio.

---

# The most important conclusion

I think you should **stop making large visual changes now**.

You've reached the point where the next gains should come from **semantic depth and controlled interaction**, not from adding more visual components.

The next evolution should be:

```text
CURRENT
Beautiful + coherent SREDSOL homepage
                 ↓
NEXT
Real EmDash content relationships
                 ↓
THEN
Small meaningful interactive systems
                 ↓
THEN
Threlte-based exploration layer
```

That is the right progression.

The site is now starting to communicate something quite distinctive:

> **SREDSOL doesn't merely make software. It constructs systems in which computation, physical reality, observation and learning can interact.**

That is the identity I'd protect from this point onward.
